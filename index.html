#1 Phishing Indicator

How to Spot a Fake Sender Address

Attackers can easily change the Display Name on an email to say "PayPal" or "Microsoft", but they cannot hide the real domain in the email header.

Anatomy of an Email Sender Header

From:
"PayPal Customer Support" < security-update @ paypal.security-alert-center.net >
⚠️ Display Name (Easily Spoofed) Anyone can set their name to "PayPal Security" or "IT Helpdesk". Never trust the display name alone!
🚩 Real Root Domain (The Truth) Look right before the `.com` or `.net`. In `paypal.security-alert-center.net`, the real owner is security-alert-center.net, NOT paypal.com!
1

Display Name Spoofing

The attacker sets the display name to a legitimate brand while using a completely random personal email address.

"Apple Support" <john8923@gmail.com>
2

Typosquatting

Swapping visually similar letters (like capital 'I' for lowercase 'l', or 'rn' for 'm') to fool your eyes at a quick glance.

service@paypaI.com (Capital 'I')
3

Subdomain Masking

Placing the real brand name as a prefix subdomain in front of a malicious root domain.

support.microsoft.com.login-verify.net

4 Other Red Flag Indicators to Watch For

1. Extreme Urgency

Threats like "Account suspended in 2 hours!" or "Immediate action required" force fast, panicked decisions.

2. Suspicious Links

Buttons that lead to shortened URLs (bit.ly) or domains that don't match the official company website.

3. Data Requests

Legitimate banks and services will never ask you for passwords, PIN codes, or 2FA tokens via email.

4. Generic Greeting

Phrases like "Dear Customer" or "Dear User" instead of your actual first and last name.

Essential Security Checklist

5 Golden Rules Before You Click

Memorize these simple rules to stay 100% protected against phishing attacks.

1

Check the Sender

Always inspect the full domain behind the @ symbol, not just the display name.

Root Domain Rule
2

Hover Before Clicking

Place your mouse over buttons or links to verify where they actually take you.

Inspect URL Preview
3

Never Share Credentials

No legit company asks for passwords, PIN codes, or 2FA codes via email or SMS.

Keep Passwords Private
4

Navigate Directly

If you get a notification, open your browser and type the URL yourself or use your official app.

Direct Access Only
5

Report Suspicious Mails

Flag suspicious messages in your email client to protect colleagues and friends.
